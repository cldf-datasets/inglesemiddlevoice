This database features the cross-linguistic sample of middle marking languages
gathered during the project “Towards a diachronic typology of the middle voice”
funded by the FWO (Research Foundation Flanders, grant 12T5320N) and hosted by
the Department of Linguistics, KU Leuven (2020-2022).

The sample is based on the combination of a variety sample of over 400 languages
(Mattiola 2020), in which 105 languages were found to feature middle markers as
defined in Inglese (2022a), and a convenience sample of additional 24 languages
known to feature middle markers from the existing literature.  The final sample
includes 129 languages, for a total of 149 middle markers.

The database has served as the empirical basis for three distinct papers, each
exploring different aspects of the typology of middle voice systems.  This
diversity of aims is reflected in the structure of the database.  Inglese
(2022a) offers a synchronic typology of middle markers, focusing on their shape
and on the range of functions (both valency related and non-valency related)
that they perform.  Inglese (2022b) addresses the interaction between middle
markers and other valency changing constructions, focusing on whether in
individual languages specific voice operations are expressed by the middle
marker, by a different construction, or by both in competition.  Finally,
Inglese (2023) charts the diachronic sources of middle markers, focusing on
reflexive and non-reflexive sources of various types.
